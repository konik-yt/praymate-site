The way to your five daily prayers

Prayer is an Android app that works out each day's prayer times on your phone, rings an alarm for every prayer, and keeps a record of how you answered.

What you see above is the app's home screen, running live in your browser. The picture follows the time of day: dawn before sunrise, day until Asr, evening until Isha, and night after that.

Today

    When the time comes

    Each prayer has its own alarm. You set it once, and it keeps ringing every day, even when the app is closed.

    1. 1The alarm rings

      Turn each prayer's alarm on or off, set it to ring at the prayer time or a few minutes after, and pick its ringtone. Fajr alarms always ring before sunrise.

    2. 2You answer it

      Tap I'm praying, or tap Delay and choose how many minutes to wait, from 1 to 120. The alarm comes back when that time is up.

    3. 3It goes in your history

      Every answer is saved, first on your phone and then to your account. The history grid colors each prayer by how many reminders it took.

    Built to be relied on

    Works offline
    Prayer times are calculated on your phone from your location. The app keeps today's and tomorrow's schedule, so it needs no connection to show them.
    Matches your local timetable
    Muslim World League is the starting method. Change the calculation method and the Asr setting in Settings to match your mosque.
    Keeps ringing in the background
    The app schedules a week of alarms ahead and restores them after your phone restarts. You don't need to open it every day.
    Hijri date over the picture
    The Islamic date moves to the next day at sunset. It uses a calculated calendar, so a local moon sighting can differ by a day.
